# Final operational DOE with secondary v2 reduced model

## Literature-driven modeling decisions

Mouret et al. show that nitrogen and temperature are the dominant levers for fermentative aroma kinetics, and that gas-liquid balances are needed to avoid confusing biological synthesis with physical evaporation. The final DOE therefore spans low, mid, and high YAN levels, includes temperature setpoint changes, uses CO2 online, and includes terminal condensate.

Morakul et al. support treating gas-liquid partitioning as composition/temperature dependent rather than estimating arbitrary loss constants from liquid data alone. The DOE keeps UNIFAC/partition parameters literature-informed and only estimates low-dimensional aroma synthesis/loss scale parameters.

Henriques/Scott dynamic genome-scale studies motivate a mechanistic interpretation for pyruvate, acetaldehyde, acetate, acetyl-CoA/redox, and ester formation. However, our fit-capacity diagnostic showed that several detailed redox/acetate-drain terms are not transferable or identifiable. The final DOE therefore uses a reduced empirical-mechanistic secondary layer.

mu0, qN, betaG0, betaF0, qEG, qEF, iG, iE, Kd0, gammaG0, gammaF0, kPyrS_N, kPyrO2, kPyrDrain, kAldS_N, kAcAld, kAcStress, k_EA_growth, k_EA_stationary, k_IAA_growth, k_IAA_stationary, k_EO_growth, k_EO_stationary, alpha_EA_loss, alpha_IAA_loss, alpha_EO_loss

## Interpretation

- This is a model-based DOE over the reduced v2 secondary layer, core fermentation states, CO2, and aroma liquid/condensate outputs.
- The campaign is not asking DOE to identify the terms that fit-capacity already showed as non-transferable.
- Synthetic experiments dominate the information matrix because composition is controllable; natural experiments are retained to test matrix transfer before MPCC use in wine must.
- All liquid samples and manual pulses are scheduled in operational windows; CO2 is online; terminal condensate retrieval may occur after the nominal horizon because the trap integrates volatile loss.

## Additional Pyomo.DoE core check

As a consistency check with the Dowling `pyomo.contrib.doe.DesignOfExperiments` workflow, the first three selected experiments were also evaluated with the discrete Pyomo model for the reduced 11-parameter core fermentation block. This does not include the secondary/aroma states, but verifies that the selected campaign is not only informative in the custom extended FIM calculation. Results were written to `pyomo_core_reduced11_checks.csv`; all three checks returned `status = ok` and rank 11 for the 11-parameter core block.

## Operational load

The schedule respects the stated constraint of at most 4 liquid samples per fermentation per day, between 10:00 and 16:00. Because each lot runs three fermentations in parallel, this corresponds to up to 12 liquid samples per calendar day at lot level. The selected campaign contains 244 liquid samples, 117 DO spot measurements, 13 manual pulses, and 9 terminal condensate retrievals.

## Literature sources used for the campaign logic

- Morakul, Mouret, Nicolle, Trelea, Sablayrolles, and Athes, 2011: gas-liquid aroma partitioning depends mainly on must composition and temperature; use literature partitioning instead of freely estimating all loss constants from liquid data alone. Source: https://agris.fao.org/search/en/providers/122535/records/65df4ff9b766d82b18012d94
- Mouret, Morakul, Nicolle, Athes, and Sablayrolles, 2012: online aroma balance shows compound-dependent gas losses and high ester losses at warm final temperature; this motivates terminal condensate and warm/high-CO2 challenge designs. Source: https://hero.epa.gov/reference/2113518
- Mouret/Godillot-style nitrogen-temperature aroma kinetics: initial nitrogen, nitrogen addition timing, and temperature jointly affect fermentation kinetics and aroma synthesis; this motivates low/mid/high YAN and timed N-rescue designs. Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2023.1124970/full
- Henriques/Scott dynamic genome-scale wine fermentation modeling: pyruvate, acetaldehyde, acetate, redox, acetyl-CoA, and ester formation are mechanistically linked, but the present data support only a reduced empirical-mechanistic secondary layer for robust DOE. Local source: `Henriques_2023.pdf`
